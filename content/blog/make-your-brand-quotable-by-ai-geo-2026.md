A growing share of your future customers will never see your website before they decide to trust you.

They'll ask an AI assistant — "best gothic streetwear brands in India," "is custom code better than Shopify for a clothing brand," "who builds D2C storefronts without WordPress" — and they'll act on the answer it gives. If your brand isn't *in* that answer, you don't exist in that conversation.

This is Generative Engine Optimization — GEO — and it's where SEO was around 2010: most people aren't doing it, the ones who are have a quiet, compounding advantage.

---

## Why classic SEO isn't enough anymore

Google SEO optimises to win a *click*. GEO optimises to be *quoted* — to become part of the model's answer, with or without a click.

The mechanics are different:

- A model can't run your JavaScript. If your content only appears after a framework hydrates, the model may never see it.
- A model rewards clarity and structure over keyword density. It's extracting facts, not ranking pages.
- A model trusts content it can parse unambiguously — clean HTML, explicit statements, structured data.

You can rank #1 on Google and still be absent from every AI answer about your category. The two are no longer the same game.

---

## The four things that make you quotable

**1. Ship an `llms.txt` file.** This is a plain-text file at the root of your domain that tells AI crawlers, in clean prose, who you are, what you offer, and what's true about you. It's the AI-era equivalent of a sitemap — a curated summary you control. Every site I build ships one. [InHimalayas](https://www.divyanshsood.com/projects/inhimalayas) saw a measurable lift in AI-referred traffic after we added llms.txt plus structured data.

**2. State facts as facts.** Models extract sentences. "Founded in 2022, we build custom-coded storefronts and have shipped 9 live client sites" is quotable. "We're passionate about crafting digital experiences" is not — it says nothing a model can repeat. Write the line you'd want quoted back to you.

**3. Add JSON-LD structured data.** Organization, Product, FAQ, Review schema. This is how you hand a machine the facts in a format it can't misread — your prices, your offerings, your ratings, your answers to common questions.

**4. Make your content server-rendered.** If a crawler has to execute JavaScript to read your page, assume some won't. Server-render or statically generate the important content so it's there in the raw HTML.

---

## Write the FAQ the model is looking for

AI assistants love a clear question-and-answer pair, because that's the shape of what users ask them.

Put a real FAQ on your key pages — product pages, your about page, your service pages. Phrase the questions the way a customer would type them into ChatGPT, and answer in one or two complete, self-contained sentences. Then wrap it in FAQ schema.

Done well, your exact answer becomes the model's answer. That's the whole game: you're not hoping to be summarised, you're handing over the summary.

---

## How to check if it's working

You don't need a fancy tool to start:

- Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity a question your ideal customer would ask. Are you mentioned? Is what they say *accurate*?
- Check your analytics referrers for `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`, and similar. That traffic is already arriving for brands that did this early.
- Fetch your own `llms.txt` and read it as if you were a stranger. Does it tell the true, complete story in plain language?
