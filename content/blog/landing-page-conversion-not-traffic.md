When sales are slow, the instinct is to buy more traffic. More ads, more spend, more clicks.

But traffic is the most expensive lever you can pull, and usually the wrong one first. If your page converts at 1% and you double traffic, you've doubled your ad bill to keep the same leak. Fix the page to 2% and you've doubled revenue for free — and *then* the ads work twice as hard.

Here's where pages actually leak, in the order they cost you the most.

---

## 1. It loads too slowly to matter

This is the leak nobody sees because it happens before anyone's looking.

On mobile, a meaningful share of visitors abandon before a slow page even renders. They tapped your ad, waited two seconds, saw nothing, and left. You paid for that click and got a bounce.

A custom-built page should hit Lighthouse 90+ on mobile as a baseline. If yours is in the 50s — common on heavy builders and plugin-stacked sites — speed *is* your conversion problem, and no headline rewrite fixes it. Test yours at [pagespeed.web.dev](https://pagespeed.web.dev) before you touch anything else.

---

## 2. The first screen doesn't say what this is

You have about five seconds. In that window a visitor decides: *am I in the right place?*

Most hero sections fail it. They lead with a clever tagline, a brand mood, or "Welcome to [company]." The visitor still doesn't know what you do or whether it's for them.

The hero needs three things, fast:

- **What it is** — in plain language, not a metaphor
- **Who it's for** — so the right person feels seen
- **One obvious next action** — a single primary button

If a stranger can't tell what you offer from the first screen alone, the headline is the leak. Not the traffic.

---

## 3. There are too many things to do

Every link is an exit. A landing page with a full nav, five CTAs, social icons, and a newsletter popup is offering a dozen ways to leave and one way to convert.

Strong pages are ruthless. One primary action, repeated. Secondary paths demoted or removed. The more focused the page, the higher it converts — counterintuitive until you watch session recordings of people clicking everything *except* the button.

---

## 4. The call-to-action creates friction

People convert in the channel they already trust. For a huge range of businesses in India, that channel is WhatsApp — not a six-field form that emails an inbox nobody checks.

When we rebuilt [Nandini Travels](https://www.divyanshsood.com/projects/nandini), the unlock wasn't traffic — they had plenty. It was giving every package a one-tap WhatsApp CTA with the details pre-filled. Bookings that used to leak to Google Maps started flowing through a site they own.

Match the CTA to how your customer actually wants to reach you. A frictionless WhatsApp tap beats a "perfect" form almost every time.

---

## 5. There's no reason to believe you

A page can be fast, clear, and focused — and still not convert, because the visitor has no reason to trust you yet.

Proof closes the gap: a real testimonial with a real name, a number you can stand behind, logos, a live result. [Modern K.B.S.](https://www.divyanshsood.com/projects/modernkbs) converts on one line — "every other agency said 6 months; delivered in 7 days." Specific, verifiable, and worth more than a paragraph of adjectives.

Vague claims ("trusted by many," "industry-leading") are invisible. Specific proof is what flips a maybe into a yes.

---

## The order that actually works

Before you raise the ad budget, run the page through this:

- ✓ Lighthouse 90+ on mobile
- ✓ A stranger knows what you do from the first screen
- ✓ One primary action, distractions removed
- ✓ CTA in the channel your customer trusts (often WhatsApp)
- ✓ Specific, believable proof above the fold

Fix the page first. Then every rupee you spend on traffic is worth roughly double — because you're no longer paying to fill a bucket with a hole in it.
